Fatima & Christ the King

Christ the King

In 1925, Pope XI wrote Quas Primas instituting the Feast of Christ the King. It was written in response to the end of monarchies, which was a direct assault on fatherhood - papacy, priesthood, and domestic fatherhood. In that same month, Jesus appeared to Sister Lucia asking for the Five First Saturday Devotion. Sister Lucia was one of the three shepherd children to whom Mary appeared on the 13th of each month from May to October 1917. On October 13, 1917, the day of the Miracle of the Sun, Saint Joseph appeared with Mary. He was holding the Child Jesus and together they blessed the entire world. This image of Joseph blessing the world shows his unmatched cooperation with and sharing in the Kingship of Christ, the Divinity of Christ, and the Mission of Christ. The Feast of Christ the King was a plea to men of faith and goodwill to restore the temporal monarchy of Jesus - the Social Kingship of Jesus.

For historical context, in February and June of 1689, Jesus, through Saint Mary Margaret Aloquoch, asked the French King Louis XIV to consecrate France to His Sacred Heart. In her letters she wrote, "He desires, then, it seems to me, to enter with pomp and magnificence into the palaces of kings and princes, therein to be honored as much as He has been despised, humiliated, and outraged in His Passion." This is likely the clearest request of Jesus to honor his Kingship both in heaven and on earth.

The king refused, along with two subsequent kings, ushering in the French Revolution which resulted in the abolition of the monarchy, execution of Louis XVI in January 1793, violent suppression of Christianity, and the rise of radical secularism and anti-clericalism. This was the beginning of the destruction of every monarchy on earth. Near the end of the destruction, Jesus sent His mother to three shepherd children in Fatima Portugal to ask for penance and reparations, especially through the Rosary.

In contrast, in 1646, King John IV placed the Portuguese royal crown on the head of Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, declaring her Queen and Patroness of Portugal. He then never wore the crown again, and no Portuguese monarch ever wore it afterward. This was a national consecration, making Portugal the only European kingdom whose monarch voluntarily surrendered the crown to the Mother of God. While France refused the Sacred Heart request, Portugal did the opposite. Queen Maria I (r. 1777–1816) promoted devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus and built the Estrela Basilica, the first church in the world dedicated to the Sacred Heart of Jesus.

Fast forward to Russia in 1917. The last monarchy fell in the February Revolution with the fall of Nicholas II – the last Tsar of Russia. Mary appeared to three children just months later in May 1917. She speaks of a world war, warns of a second that would be worse, if people did not repent and amend their ways. She also asked for the Consecration of Russia to her Immaculate Heart. Then in October 1917, two events converged – the Miracle of the Sun and the Bolshevik Revolution (aka October Revolution). Much has been written on the errors of Russia, but little has been written about the rejection of Christ the King.

In Russia, the Romanovs are executed in 1918, Russia becomes officially atheist and violently anti-Christian. In Europe, the Austro‑Hungarian monarchy collapses in November 1918. Emperor Karl I of Austria — a devout Catholic — is exiled. He dies in poverty in 1922. The German and Ottoman monarchies fall in 1918. Both were traditional Christian monarchies. By 1925, nearly every traditional Christian monarchy is gone. This is the same year Pope Pius XI institutes the feast of Christ the King. In his ___ Quas Primas he explicitly counters secularism, communism, the dethroning of Christian rulers, and the denial of Christ’s kingship over society.

Nicholas II is canonized by the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000. Karl I is beatified by the Catholic Church in 2004.

The apparition’s warning about Russia is not merely about communism — it is about the spiritual dethroning of Christ in public life. This is precisely what the Social Kingship of Christ addresses.

Why Portugal? The last monarch of Portugal was Manuel II, who reigned from 1908 to 1910, when the monarchy was abolished and Portugal became a republic in the Republican Revolution of 1910. Portugal is unique among former Catholic monarchies because it formally consecrated the nation to the Blessed Virgin Mary and symbolically gave its crown to Our Lady — something no other European monarchy ever did. This act created a distinctive national relationship with Christ and the Sacred Heart through Mary.

Saint Joseph's presence at Fatima is significant. First, as the legal lineage of King David, it is through Joseph that Jesus inherits the temporal earthly throne. Jesus blessing the world with Joseph ratifies the theology of all for Jesus through Mary with Joseph. Second, this is a dire warning. As this project has reflected on, Mary is the Mother of Mercy whereas Joseph is the just father – mirror and complementarity of mercy and justice. Mary can only withhold the wrath of God for so long. As Mary told Sister Sasagawa on October 13, 1973:

If men do not repent and improve, the Father will inflict a punishment greater than the Flood... With my Son I have intervened so many times to appease the wrath of the Father. I have prevented the coming of calamities by offering Him the sufferings of His Son on the Cross, His Precious Blood, and the souls who console Him.

In the same year that Pope Pius instituted the Feast of Christ The King, Jesus and His Mother appeared to Sister Lucia requesting the Five First Saturday Devotion in reparations for sins against the Immaculate Heart of Mary. The Five First Saturdays consist of (1) going to confession, (2) receiving Holy Communion, (3) saying five decades of the Rosary, (4) meditating for fifteen minutes on one or more of the mysteries of the Rosary, and (5) with the intent of making reparations to the Immaculate Heart of Mary.

This devotion is perfectly suited for the working father. The minimum is one Saturday for five consecutive months. As the devotion has grown, accommodations have been allowed, making even more suited for workers. Even if one must skip a Saturday or forgets one of the requirements, the grace is still there. As Saint Jacinta said, "God grants graces through the Immaculate Heart of Mary. They must ask for them from her." This is the first step in reestablishing the Social Kingship of Christ – fathers enthroning Jesus in their homes, the domestic church.
