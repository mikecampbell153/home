Thesis: Josephology as a Protection Against Freemasonic Error

Josephology safeguards the Church from the core theological errors of Freemasonry by restoring the incarnational, covenantal, and Christological meaning of “architecture” and “foundation.” Freemasonry’s “Great Architect of the Universe” is a deistic, non‑incarnate, religiously‑indifferent construct of human reason. Josephology, by contrast, reveals Saint Joseph as the human father whose vocational architecture is entirely derivative of, ordered toward, and dependent upon the Incarnate Son.

Just as Mariology protected the Church from early Christological heresies by clarifying the identity of Christ through the identity of His Mother, Josephology protects the Church from modern deistic and naturalistic errors by clarifying the identity of Christ through the identity of His earthly father.

The historical rise of Freemasonry (17th–18th century) and the theological emergence of Josephology (19th–20th century) form a meaningful correlation: as rationalist movements attempted to replace revelation with natural religion, the Church deepened reflection on the hidden life of Jesus and the vocational fatherhood of Joseph. Josephology thus stands as a modern doctrinal counterweight to Freemasonry’s denial of revelation, incarnation, and ecclesial authority.
