Years ago—back when Google and Twitter were still emerging—I searched my own name and found it buried somewhere around page twenty. With Michael holding a fifty‑year streak as the most common first name and Campbell sitting as the seventh most common surname, I was effectively competing against millions of digital doppelgängers.

So I set a simple challenge: get to page one.

My strategy wasn’t sophisticated. I posted consistently on Twitter and pointed everything back to my blog. That alone was enough to climb the rankings. Then the algorithm shifted, work accelerated, and the blog went quiet.

Before it did, I tracked traffic closely. My most‑read post was on the fraud triangle, which will make its way into a future piece. Fast‑forward to today: after the sale of Venminder and the pace finally slowing, I’ve found space for a professional hobby. I’m also at a stage in my career where I’m doing fewer hands‑on technical projects, so keeping those skills sharp matters.

That’s led to a renewed digital strategy built on three pillars:
• LinkedIn Content Calendar — consistent, structured posting
• Josephology Substack — long‑form theological work
• GitHub Projects — PowerBI, PowerQuery‑M, DAX, and SQL

It’s not about page one anymore. It’s about intentional presence, professional stewardship, and keeping the tools of the trade sharp.
