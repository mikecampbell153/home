Slide - Prodigal Son

The story of The Prodigal Son is a story of a father and his two sons. The younger son is known as the prodigal son. He leaves home, squanders his inheritance on loose living, and then returns home after hitting rock bottom and repenting of his sins. The elder son stays home, is obedient to his father, but gets angry when his brother returns.

Read the story

Luke 15:11 And he said, "There was a man who had two sons; 12 and the younger of them said to his father, 'Father, give me the share of property that falls to me.' And he divided his living between them. 13 Not many days later, the younger son gathered all he had and took his journey into a far country, and there he squandered his property in loose living. 14 And when he had spent everything, a great famine arose in that country, and he began to be in want. 15 So he went and joined himself to one of the citizens of that country, who sent him into his fields to feed swine. 16 And he would gladly have fed on[a] the pods that the swine ate; and no one gave him anything. 17 But when he came to himself he said, 'How many of my father's hired servants have bread enough and to spare, but I perish here with hunger! 18 I will arise and go to my father, and I will say to him, "Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you; 19 I am no longer worthy to be called your son; treat me as one of your hired servants."' 20 And he arose and came to his father. But while he was yet at a distance, his father saw him and had compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him. 21 And the son said to him, 'Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you; I am no longer worthy to be called your son.'[b] 22 But the father said to his servants, 'Bring quickly the best robe, and put it on him; and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet; 23 and bring the fatted calf and kill it, and let us eat and make merry; 24 for this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found.' And they began to make merry.

25 "Now his elder son was in the field; and as he came and drew near to the house, he heard music and dancing. 26 And he called one of the servants and asked what this meant. 27 And he said to him, 'Your brother has come, and your father has killed the fatted calf, because he has received him safe and sound.' 28 But he was angry and refused to go in. His father came out and entreated him, 29 but he answered his father, 'Lo, these many years I have served you, and I never disobeyed your command; yet you never gave me a kid, that I might make merry with my friends. 30 But when this son of yours came, who has devoured your living with harlots, you killed for him the fatted calf!' 31 And he said to him, 'Son, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours. 32 It was fitting to make merry and be glad, for this your brother was dead, and is alive; he was lost, and is found.'"

Talk

The younger son asks for his father's inheritance. An inheritance is typically received at death. So to ask for it in advance can be akin to saying that I cannot wait for you to die. Out of the arrogance of premature autonomy, the younger son thinks that he can do better on his own. As Proverb teaches, Pride comes before the fall.

The son chose himself over his father. He wanted the gifts without the giver. It is a reminder of the original sin committed in the garden of Eden -- wanting to be God without God. And without God's grace, we can do nothing. Original sin leaves us inclined to the world, the flesh, and the devil. As we heard in Joe's talk, Satan tempts us with the flesh (hunger), the world (power), and the devil (don't worship God).

The father honors the son's wish and gives him half of the inheritance and the older son the other half. Instead of thinking 50/50 split, we can think of it as the father giving his sons everything that he has. He holds nothing back. The analogy is God the Father, gives us everything, even His only begotten Son. God cannot be divided and His gifts are infinite.

For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son the whosoever believes in Him shall have eternal life.

God is so generous. In the sacraments of initiation, baptism and confirmation, we receive the gifts of the Holy Spirit: Fear of God, Wisdom, Piety, Counsel, Knowledge, Understanding, and Fortitude. At confirmation, we receive an increase in these gifts.

Slide - Pentecost

We don't talk a lot about Confirmation, which can be called the Sacrament of the Holy Spirit. The Catechism uses the term "divine self-effacement" in talking about the Holy Spirit, quote, "No one comprehends the thoughts of God except the Spirit of God." The Holy Spirit, reveals God, makes known to us Jesus, but the Spirit does not speak of himself. He has spoken through the prophets. We know him only in the movement. Like the wind, we here its movement in the trees, but we not hear the wind itself.

It reminds a quote by Saint Bernadette, "The Blessed Virgin used me like a broom. What do you do with a broom when you have finished sweeping? You put it back in its place, behind the door!" This was her way of rejecting notoriety and insisting on humility despite the global attention the Lourdes apparitions brought. She wished to remain humble and obedient. She just wanted to be an instrument in the hand of God, Who is the source of grace, not the saint.

Similarly, Mother Teresa said , "I'm a little pencil in the hand of a writing God, who is sending a love letter to the world." The saints do not speak of themselves, they point us to God.

Through Confirmation, we are sealed with the gift of the Holy Spirit, which (1) rooted more deeply as Children of the Father; (2) united more firmly to Christ; and (3) increases the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

The first gift is Fear of God, which leads to Wisdom, or the culmination of all seven gifts. Fear of God is not a servile fear as a slave to his master, but a respect and awe for the power of God. God is completely other. God created the universe out of nothing. God is outside His creation and cannot be contained by it. The Catechism states, "God is infinitely greater than all his works. God's greatness is unsearchable. (CCC 300)

While God transcends creation and is also present to it. God is present to his creatures' inmost being: "In him we live and move and have our being." God is a personal God; a Trinity of three Divine Persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. We are children of God not only because He created us but because He created us in His image. God is love.

Slide - Prodigal Son #2

Back to the story. The son receives his inheritance from the father and after a few days, he goes off to a distant land. He leaves his father's house with all of the gifts that come along with that. Not all homes are loving, nurturing environments, but in this story, since the father represents God, then we can rightly imagine that his house was full of warmth, protection, good food, and love.

Spiritually, the son walked away from the gifts that God gives us. He walked away from the grace of God. He turned his back on his faith. The far off land is an image of walking away from God. As the son leaves the house, an image of leaving the Church. To use Saint Augustine's analogy, leaving the City of God and for the City of Man. Saint Augustine goes on to say, "The far country is forgetfulness of God, a forgetfulness which is mutual, for inasmuch as the sinner forgets God, God in His turn is in a manner forgetful of the sinner, that is, God ceases to bestow on him light, grace, or guidance." (Lapide)

Notice Saint Luke writes that only after a few days does the son leave home. By analogy, one may be sitting in the pew but his or her heart is far from God. If not redirected, they leave the Church and descend farther from the grace of the Church and her sacraments.

Upon arriving at the far off country, the son squandered everything that he had on loose living. After spending everything, there was a famine in the land and he began to get desperate. This is what sin does. It results in a loss of God's gifts of graces. Not only gifts of grace but even gifts of nature. God has written His law on our hearts. To live in sin is to reject the dignity of own human nature.

Once the heart is far from God, it tries to fill that void with worldly pleasures. Saint Augustine says that our hearts are restless until it finds rest in God. Nothing satisfies the soul, leaving the soul vulnerable to the point that one is willing to sell his soul to the devil. Said another way, he sowed his wild oats, had the time of his life, what happens in Vegas stays in Vegas. It stops satisfying the flesh, it loses its luster, leaving one lonely, depressed, and empty.

Then famine occurs. Famine, or desert, is a consistent theme through salvation history. Adam and Eve are banished from the Garden. The Israelites wander in the desert for forty years before returning to the promised land. Jesus is tempted for forty days in the desert. God allows these chastisements through His grace and mercy because they can bring us back to Him, if we have a humble and contrite heart.

Today, we are in the 40-days of lent. We voluntarily enter into forty days of fasting to bring us closer to God as we prepare for the Passion, Death, and Resurrection or our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

In this time of famine, the prodigal joined with a citizen of that country who sent him to feed the swines. The citizen of the far off country is none other than the prince of this world, Satan. Then situation got so bad that the son longed to eat what the pigs eat. He went from citizen into an alien, from a son into a hireling, from a rich man into a beggar, from a free man into a slave. (Lapide)

To long for the slop that pigs may be the absolute lowest point that a Jewish person can hit. Pigs were considered unclean and to eat their slop is debasing one to be even lower than a pig.

But just look at how loving, how generous, how merciful is our God. He enters into our brokenness. He enters into our despair. He enters into our mess. He entered into this world in the most vulnerable fashion, an infant. In supreme humility He was born of the Virgin Mary who, "gave birth to a son and wrapped him in swaddling cloths, and laid him in a manger." ... "our Mother of Mercy laid him In a manger." ... He entered into the world exactly where this poor prodigal son longed to eat. No matter how messy or sloppy we make of our lives, Jesus is waiting and wanting to enter into our lives.

Slide - Nativity Scene

He finally comes to his senses. The gift of counsel, one of the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit, is supernatural common sense. Sin darkens our intellect. Sin darkens our ability to use common sense. Common sense tells us that sin, while pleasurable at first, is a slippery slope to more sin, and eventually misery. Bad habits become addictions and addictions become all-consuming.

God always provides a way out. One way are programs like AA that provide a structured plan to recovery. And for good reason because these programs are built on taking responsibility and admitting wrong-doing that only be accomplished by God's grace. Then cooperating with God's grace and working through steps to recovery and reconciliation.

The son says, "I will arise and go to my father, and I will say to him, "Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you; I am no longer worthy to be called your son; treat me as one of your hired servants."'

He says to himself, I will arise, because when you've hit rock bottom, the only way to look is up. He has true contrition recognizing that he has sinned not only against his fellow man, but also against the Heavenly Father. He is detached from his life of sin and now has contempt things of this world. Conversion of the heart is the first step in our journey back to God.

The Catechism reads, "God created us without us: but he did not will to save us without us. To receive his mercy, we must admit our faults. If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, and will forgive our sins and cleanse us from all unrighteousness." (CCC 1847)

"Through the gifts of the Church, Jesus, our divine physician has given us the Sacraments of Healing -- Confession and Anointing of the Sick. During Jesus' earthly ministry, he forgave sins. Mary Magdalene from whom seven demons were cast out was filled with peace and loved much. The paralytic who was lowered through a roof by friends. The woman caught in adultery to whom He said go and sin no more. The thief on the Cross known as Saint Dismas. Jesus prayed from the Cross "forgive them for they know not what they do." (cf. USCCA)

"Only God can forgive sins, but Jesus willed that the Church should be the instrument of forgiveness on earth. On the night of that first Easter Sunday, Jesus breathed on the apostles and said, "As the Father has sent me, so I send you ... Receive the Holy Spirit. Whose sins you forgive are forgiven them, and whose sins you retain are retained." (John 20:21-23) That night Jesus gave the Church the ministry of forgiveness of sins through the apostles.

Slide Divine Mercy

The priest sits in persona Christi. Just like all of the other sacraments and sacramentals, we have something tangible, or incarnate, to fully experience Divine Mercy. We can hear the words from the priest, who is sitting there, not merely in the place of Jesus, but in union with Christ. When he speaks in the first person, it is the priest who speaks as well as Christ, who say, I absolve you from your sins. We know that it is truly Jesus who is forgiving our sins through His Church. He will never leave us nor abandon us. He has remained with us in the Eucharist and in the Church.

"Confession liberates us from sins that trouble our hearts and makes it possible to be reconciled to God and others." How many of us practice our confessions before we go. Father forgive me, it's been x months, or years, since my last confession. During that time I committed this sin a dozen time and that sin a few times. I am sorry for these sins and all my other sins. I can imagine this son writing out his confession in his mind, until he finally has the courage to head home. And on his way home, he rehearses over and over. Father forgive me for I have sinned.

The last thing I want to point out in this passage is that the son said he was not worthy of being called a son and was content to be a servant. It reminds me of the centurion who we quote at every Mass, "I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof, but only say the word and my soul shall be healed." Again, God is Love, He is personal, and He wants us to be who created us to be, His children.

Next, we get to see how the father in this story restores the sonship of his wayward child.

But while the prodigal son was yet at a distance, his father saw him and had compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him.

This verse is so rich with the depth of God's love and mercy. While he was still at a distance, he ran. How long had the father been waiting for his son to return home? Did he go out there every day to look for his son? Or Did he wait there day and night looking for his son? Of course, this would be impossible for a man, but what is impossible for man is possible with God, Who is infinite. He is not bound by time nor space. He waits for each of us to return to him. He is waiting with love and mercy for those who repent and go to Him.

His father saw him and had compassion. I heard a priest say that one of his jobs is to remind people of who they truly are, which is redeemed. At our baptism, we became a new creation. In the waters of baptism, we died to sin, and arose with new life. Holiness is not something to grasped like fruit on a tree. Holiness is a gift from God, received by His grace and our cooperation with His grace. And everyting the Church does is order to our holiness, most especially the sacraments.

Philippians 2:5-8 reads ... Christ Jesus, who, though he was in the form of God, did not consider equality with God something to be grasped, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form he humbled himself and became obedient unto death, even death on a cross.

Lastly, the father ran to him. This Jewish patriarch ran. In excess of love and joy, he did not wait for the son to get closer, but ran to meet him. Running shows the greatest act of his love. In the Scriptures we never read of a patriarch running. Modern scholarship recognizes how unusual this is for a Jewish father to run. They typically wore long tunics. So to run, they would have to pull up the tunic and expose their legs, which was considered immodest and undignified. For him to run was an act not only of extreme love and joy, but also an act of humility.

The father embraced and kissed his son, physical signs of pardon and reconciliation. A picture of the joy in heaven as the angels rejoice over one sinner who returns. All of this happens before the son makes his confession. Then after confessing, he is restored to sonship. The son would have settled for being a hired hand, but the father wanted to restore him to full sonship.

First, he gives his son a robe. The symbolism is that we are clothed with Christ.

Second, he puts a ring on his finger a symbol symbol of the Holy Spirit. The signet ring of kings was used as a seal. At our confirmation we are sealed with the gift of Holy Spirit.

Third, he puts shoes on his feet because slaves go barefoot, not children of God. Saint Paul teaches, "And because you are sons, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, "Abba! Father!" So through God you are no longer a slave but a son, and if a son then an heir."

It is only after all of this that the feast can begin. The fattened calf is slaughtered. It is a reminder of going to confession and being in a state of grace before receiving the Eucharist. The fattened calf is a symbol of our Savior Jesus Christ, who was crucified for our salvation. The calf was fattened, a symbol of the sufficiency of Christ's sacrifice made once and for all. Because He is fully God and fully man, He fully paid the price for all of our sins. On the cross He cried out, "It is finished".

The son enters under the Father's roof and joins in the feast, eating and celebrating. The Eucharist is our heavenly banquet on earth.

Slide Joseph & Jacob

The story does not end there. We now turn to the older son. Not unlike Joseph receiving a robe from his father Jacob causing jealousy among his brothers, the older son became angry and refused to join the banquet. Just like the younger son, we find him outside of the house, and the father going out to greet him. We don't know the end of his story. I hope that he listens to his father, examines his conscience, and returns to the house.

Another observation is that the younger son is not there. Sin results in broken relationships. Although our sins are completely forgiven, they still carry temporal consequences. Relationships need mending, habits need broken. This is a reason why we exchange the sign of peace at Mass before receiving Eucharist.

In the sermon on the mount Jesus taught, "If you are offering your gift at the altar, and there remember that your brother has something against you, leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift."
