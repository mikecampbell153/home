    • Self-effacement - the quality of not claiming attention for oneself; the act or fact of keeping oneself in the background, as in humility

The Holy Spirit reveals God and makes known to us Christ, but the Spirit does not speak of himself. The Spirit has spoken through the prophets, but we do not hear the Spirit himself. We know him only in the movement by which he reveals the Word to us. The Spirit of truth who "unveils" Christ to us "will not speak on his own." The Catechism calls this divine self-effacement. (CCC 687)

Mary, Spouse of the Holy Spirit (CCC 721-726)

  • "Mary, the all-holy ever-virgin Mother of God, is the masterwork of the mission of the Son and the Spirit in the fullness of time... Mary is acclaimed and represented in the liturgy as the "Seat of Wisdom". (CCC 721)
  • "I am the mother of fair love, and of fear, and of knowledge, and of holy hope. In me is all grace of the way and of the truth, in me is all hope of life and of virtue." (Sirach 24:24-25)
  • This is why she is rightly called Most Gracious Advocate and Mediatrix of all Grace. (CCC 969)
  • The Holy Spirit prepared Mary by his grace. It was fitting ... she should be "full of grace." She was, by sheer grace, conceived without sin as the most humble of creatures, the most capable of welcoming the inexpressible gift of the Almighty. (CCC 722)
  • Through the Holy Spirit, the Virgin conceives and gives birth to the Son of God. (CCC 723)
  • She is the burning bush of the definitive theophany. Filled with the Holy Spirit she makes the Word visible in the humility of his flesh. (CCC 724)
  • "Finally, through Mary, the Holy Spirit begins to bring men, the objects of God's merciful love, into communion with Christ. And the humble are always the first to accept him... Mary become the Woman, the new Eve ("mother of the living"), the mother of the "whole Christ". [cf John 19:25-27] As such, she was present with the Twelve, who "with one accord devoted themselves to prayer,"... which the Spirit was to inaugurate on the morning of Pentecost with the manifestation of the Church. (CCC 725-726)

Seven Gifts of the Holy Spirit (CCC 1831)

  • Fear of the Lord
    • Foundational gift
    • Reverent awe of God in His majesty and justice
    • Holy fear that leads one to avoid sin (out of love not fear of punishment)
    • Detachment from the world and directed toward Heaven
    • Conversion, repentance, growth in virtue begins
  • Piety
    • Fosters a filial love for God and a joyful devotion to Him.
    • Delight in worship
    • Desire to live a life pleasing to God.
  • Knowledge
    • Allows one to see created things in their proper relation to God.
    • It helps the soul understand the world not as an end in itself, but as a means to glorify God.
  • Fortitude
    • Strength to endure trials and remain steadfast in faith.
    • Courage to pursue holiness even in the face of adversity.
  • Counsel
    • Discern the right course of action, especially in complex or morally ambiguous situations.
    • Divine guidance that leads toward salvation.
    • Prudence
    • Supernatural common sense
  • Understanding
    • Spiritual vision is purified and allows one to perceive divine truths more clearly.
    • Spiritual insight requires moral and spiritual cleansing.
    • Saint Augustine - "That I may know You, may I know myself."
  • Wisdom
    • The highest of the gifts, wisdom enables the soul to contemplate divine things and to love them above all else.
    • It is the culmination of the spiritual journey.

Rite of Confirmation

Acts 8:14-17 Now when the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had accepted the word of God, they sent them Peter and John, who went down and prayed for them, that they might receive the holy Spirit, for it had not yet fallen upon any of them; they had only been baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. Then they laid hands on them and they received the holy Spirit.

  • Sacrament of initiation
  • Increases grace received at baptism
  • Can only be received once
  • Recipient - Baptized person
  • Minister
    • Bishop (Ordinary)
    • Priest (Extraordinary)
  • Form - Be sealed with the Holy Spirit
  • Matter - Chrism Oil
  • Laying on of hands

Five Effects of Confirmation (CCC 1303)
(Increase and deepening of Baptismal Grace)

  1. Roots us in Divine Filiation (Abba Father)
  2. Unites us more firmly to Christ
  3. Increase the gifts of the Holy Spirit in us;
  4. Unites us with the Church more perfectly;
  5. Gives us special strength of the Holy Spirit
