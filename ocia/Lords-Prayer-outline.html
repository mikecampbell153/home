Outline Summary of "The Lord’s Prayer" Document

Catechism

WHAT IS PRAYER? (CCC 2559)

  • Raising of one's mind and heart to God.
    • Ascension of the soul
    • Jacob's Ladder
    • Lectio Divina
  • Requesting of good things from God
  • "Prayer is the living relationship of the children of God with their Father who is good beyond measure, with his Son Jesus Christ and with the Holy Spirit"

THE BATTLE OF PRAYER (CCC 2275)

  • Prayer is both
    • a gift of grace and
    • a determined response on our part (i.e. requires effort)
  • Against whom?
    • Against ourselves
    • against the wiles of the tempter
  • We pray as we live, we live as we pray (habits).
  • "Spiritual battle" is inseparable from the battle of prayer.

FACING DIFFICULTIES IN PRAYER (CCC 2729)

  • The habitual difficulty in prayer is distraction.
  • To set about hunting down distractions would be to fall into their trap, when
  • all that is necessary is to turn back to our heart:
  • for a distraction reveals to us what we are attached to, and
  • this humble awareness before the Lord should awaken our preferential love for him and lead us resolutely to offer him our heart to be purified.
  • Therein lies the battle, the choice of which master to serve.

I. Introduction: The Lord’s Prayer as the Heart of Christian Life

  • The Lord’s Prayer is described as "the summary of the whole Gospel" and "the fundamental Christian prayer" taught by Jesus.
  • Emphasis on praying it intentionally rather than reciting it mindlessly.
  • Encouragement to "own" the prayer - "prayer is not prayers"
  • Practical advice: find a sacred, consistent time for prayer; avoid rushed or distracted moments.
  • Practical advice: each petition stands alone and can be prayed throughout the day

II. Why We Pray the Our Father Each Week

  • It is one of the earliest prayers Christians learn.
  • It holds primacy because Jesus himself taught it (Matthew 6:9–13; Luke 11:2–4).
  • It is the Most Perfect Prayer containing the full Gospel.
  • Its weekly use in liturgy reflects its foundational role.

III. Structure of the Lord’s Prayer

  • After addressing the Father, the prayer contains seven petitions:
    • Three "Thy" petitions:
      1. Thy name
      2. Thy kingdom
      3. Thy will
    • Four "Us" petitions:
      1. Give us
      2. Forgive us
      3. Lead us not
      4. Deliver us
  • Each petition is then explained in detail.

IV. Explanation of Each Petition

1. "Our Father, who art in heaven…"

  • Jesus teaches us to pray "Our" Father, emphasizing communal identity in Christ.
  • Calling God "Father" reflects intimacy and discipleship.
  • humility is the foundation of prayer,
    • With humble and contrite heart
    • He who humbles himself will be exalted;
  • With Confidence
  • Father from all eternity.
    • Trinity revealed by Jesus
    • Confirmed at Pentecost

2. "Hallowed be thy name…"

  • God’s name is already holy; we ask that it be recognized as holy through His works.
  • God is Holy. He is completely other. He is outside of His own creation and cannot be contained by it.
  • Yet He reveals Himself to us. He is Trinity. He is personal.
  • This is a review from one of the first few OCIA sessions.

3. "Thy kingdom come…"

  • Twofold meaning:
    • God’s kingdom breaking into daily life now.
    • Christ’s final return at the end of time.
  • The Church is Jesus' Kingdom on earth.
  • We talked about this in one session on the four last things.

4. "Thy will be done…"

  • A prayer of conformity to God’s will, echoing Jesus in Gethsemane.
  • Implies willingness to take up our cross.
  • The analogy of spousal union, becoming one with God, is when the two wills become one.

5. "Give us this day our daily bread…"

  • "Daily bread" includes:
    • Physical sustenance.
    • Spiritual nourishment: Scripture and the Eucharist.

6. "Forgive us our trespasses…"

  • We ask mercy for our sins.
  • God’s forgiveness penetrates our hearts to the degree we forgive others.
  • Jesus teaches us to leave the altar and be reconciled with your brother. Sign of Peace in Mass

7. "Lead us not into temptation…"

  • God does not tempt (James 1:13).
  • We ask to be spared the path that leads to sin and near occasions of sin.
  • God gives us the Grace to avoid the occasions of sins, or that which leads us to sin.
  • It is better to prevent disease rather than need to be cured from it.
  • Said in a positive light, to grow in virtue.
  • Again, we discuessed virtues and see how the Our Father contains the whole Gospel.

8. "Deliver us from evil…"

  • "Evil" refers to Satan, "the Evil One." (CCC 2851)
  • Trust in Christ’s victory over evil.

9. "Amen!"

  • A declaration of belief: "So be it!"

V. Why Some Christians Add a Final Line

  • The doxology "For the kingdom, the power…" appears in the Didache, not in the Gospels.
  • Catholics include it in the liturgy after a priestly prayer to honor tradition while preserving the Gospel text.

VI. St. Thomas Aquinas: Five Qualities of Prayer in the Lord’s Prayer

1. Confidence

  • Prayer must be confident (Heb 4:16).
  • The Lord’s Prayer is trustworthy because it was formed by Christ.
  • St. Cyprian: "We use the very words of our Advocate."

2. Suitability

  • Prayer must ask for what is truly good.
  • We often "ask amiss" (James 4:3).
  • Christ teaches us what to desire and therefore what to pray for.

3. Order

  • Prayer expresses desire; desires must be rightly ordered.
  • Spiritual goods come before material ones (Matt 6:33).

4. Devotion

  • Devotion makes prayer pleasing to God.
  • Jesus warns against "much speaking" (Matt 6:7).
  • The Lord’s Prayer is short to preserve fervor.

5. Humility

  • God hears the humble (Ps 102:18).
  • True humility expects everything from God, not from oneself.

VII. Three Effects of Prayer (Aquinas)

1. Remedy Against Evils

  • Prayer delivers from past sin, fear of future sin, sadness, and persecution.
  • Examples: the Publican, the thief on the cross.

2. Obtaining What We Desire

  • "All things whatsoever you ask… believe that you shall receive" (Mark 11:24).
  • Unanswered prayer may reflect lack of perseverance or asking for what harms us.

3. Friendship with God

  • Prayer rises like incense (Ps 141:2).
  • It draws the soul into communion with God.
