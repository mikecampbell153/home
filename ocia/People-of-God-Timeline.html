Persian Empire (Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther, 539-331 BC)
Cyrus the Great conquers Babylon (539 BC)
Ezra 1. Cyrus permits Jews to return (538 BC)
Ezra 3. Altar rebuilt and Temple foundations laid
Ezra 4. Work halted because of opposition
Hag. 1-2. Haggai calls people to rebuild the Temple
Zech. 1-14. Zechariah encourages restoration and foretells the Messiah
Ezra 6. Second Temple completed (516 BC)
Esther. Queen Esther saves the Jewish people (c. 480-470 BC)
Ezra 7-10. Ezra returns and reforms worship and observance of the Law
Neh. 1-6. Nehemiah rebuilds Jerusalem's walls (445 BC)
Neh. 8. Ezra publicly proclaims the Law
Note. Judaism emerges with renewed focus on Temple, Torah, and synagogue

Greek Empire (Daniel, 1 Maccabees, 331-167 BC)
Dan. 7-12. Visions of successive world kingdoms
331 BC. Alexander the Great conquers Persia
Greek language and culture spread throughout the Mediterranean
323 BC. Alexander dies; empire divided among successors
Seleucid rulers eventually control Judea
167 BC. Antiochus IV Epiphanes desecrates the Temple
Note. "Abomination of Desolation" foretold in Daniel

Maccabean Period (Hasmonean Kingdom) (1 & 2 Maccabees; 167-63 BC)
1 Macc. 2. Mattathias begins revolt against Seleucids
1 Macc. 3-4. Judas Maccabeus defeats Syrian forces
1 Macc. 4. Temple purified and rededicated (Hanukkah)
Jewish independence restored
Hasmonean dynasty rules Judea for about a century
Growing factions emerge: Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes

Roman Empire (63 BC - NT era)
63 BC. Pompey captures Jerusalem
37 BC. Herod the Great becomes king of Judea
Birth of Mary
Herod greatly expands the Second Temple
John the Baptist prepares the way for Christ
Birth of Jesus Christ
Public ministry, death, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus
Pentecost and birth of the Church
70 AD. Romans destroy the Second Temple
Note. Temple sacrifices cease; Christianity and Rabbinic Judaism emerge as distinct paths
