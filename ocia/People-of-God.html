Salvation History

Intro

Road to Emmaus (Luke 24:27)

The Pentateuch comprises the first five books of the Old Testament. It depicts a series of beginnings—the beginning of the world, of humankind, and of God's promise to the Israelites.

Genesis, the first book, opens with God's creation of the world. The perfect world falls into evil when humans disobey God, and the human population divides into separate nations and languages.

There are many covenants in the Bible, some between people and some between God and people. Covenant comes from the Latin word "convenire" which means to come together. A covenant is an agreement between people, or between God and His people, which binds one to the other. This is vastly different to a contract, which involves the exchange of goods or services for money.

A covenant can be thought of as a sacred family bond, taken with an oath before a higher power, whereas a contract can be thought of as an agreement to pay someone for services rendered, which involves a promise to each other, rather than an oath. BIG DIFFERENCE. Covenants most often involve a ceremony with witnesses, invoking God, and a communal meal. The covenant therefore unites flesh and blood, as well as ensuring a common family name.

There are five covenants in the Old Testament which are fulfilled in the New Testament, or New Covenant when Jesus establishes the Church as the Kingdom of God. Each covenant in the Old Testament expands the relationships from one couple, Adam & Eve, to one family, Noah, to 12 tribes Abraham, to one nation, Moses, and finally one kingdom, David.

The Old Testament is one cohesive story with many pivotal chapters of God revealing Himself to man through covenants that culminate in a kingdom. Like any good story, there is plenty of conflict and resolutions to the conflict. It is one redemption arc from Adam to Jesus, the new Adam. Within this one overarching arc, there are many other redemption arcs with captivating and compelling stories.

These arcs follow the mysteries of the Rosary, the Life of Christ... Joyful, Sorrowful, and Glorious. Faith, sin, and redemption. Time and time again, God comes to meet man, man sins and falls away from God, and God redeems man. Hence the image of a mountain. God comes to meet man on earth while man climbs the mountain to meet God. This is the definition of prayer... Raising the heart to God.

Matthew 16:18-19 Upon this rock, I will build my church... I will give you the keys to the kingdom.

Adam & Eve (Genesis 1-5; CCC 396-421)

Genesis 1 and 2 provide two different accounts of Creation.

Genesis One provides an account in which Creation took place over six days plus a day of rest. This day of rest is important in the history of salvation because it establishes God's covenant with man. It is not explicitly stated but the idea of rest and the number seven unfold as symbols of covenant.

This first account of creation has beautiful symmetry with two sets of three. The first three; light, sea, and land are filled by the second set sun/moon, fish/birds, and animals/man. The sixth day, the day that man is created, is called very good.

The sixth day is very good because of the creation of man, which is expanded on in the second creation account. The first creation account ends on the seventh day with God resting, establishing the Sabbath Day. It ends on a high note. "And he blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made." (Gen 2:3)

The second creation account is focused on the creation of man, male and female. God created man from the dust of the earth, breathed life into him, and he became of living soul (2:7). God placed the man in the Garden of Eden, which is described as paradise with plants, herbs, trees, and flowing rivers. There are two trees mentioned specifically, the tree of life and the tree of knowledge. God told Adam that he could eat of any tree, except the tree of knowledge of good and evil. Then the Lord God said, "It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him a helper fit for him." (2:18) But no suitable helper fit for him. So God put Adam into a deep sleep and from his rib, He created woman. Adam said, "This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man."

Then the devil enters the picture. God allows this test of obedience because we are made in his image and likeness with intellect and free will. Love is not love unless it is free. God will never violate our free will and coerce us to do anything. The serpent lied to Eve and she ate of the forbidden fruit. She gave it to Adam, and he ate as well. Original sin.

God's response to sin is that He comes to meet us (the Incarnation), He provides a means of repentance (the Passion), and a plan of salvation (the Resurrection). God curses the serpent putting enmity between he and the woman, his offspring and hers. We will see this enmity throughout Salvation History. We will see these two themes throughout Salvation History. Faith, Fall or sin, Forgiveness or redemption. But not all seek forgiveness resulting is separation between City of God and City of Man, as Saint Augustine describes it.

By grace, God banished Adam and Eve from the Garden of Eden and put an angel to protect the garden. This was to prevent them from eating from the tree of life and living forever in a state of sin.

Immediately after the fall, we see in the first generation after Adam and Eve, with their first two sons, the effects of sin. Abel's sacrifice is accepted but Cain's is not. And this made him angry. God tells Cain, "Why are you angry, and why has your countenance fallen? 7 If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin is couching at the door; its desire is for you, but you must master it." Instead Cain kills Abel. Instead of turning from sin and repenting, Cain's anger grew to the point of killing his own brother, Abel.

Noah (Genesis 6-9; CCC 56-58)

When men began to multiply on the face of the ground, and daughters were born to them, the sons of God saw that the daughters of men were fair; and they took to wife such of them as they chose... The Lord saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually. 6 And the Lord was sorry that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him to his heart. 7 So the Lord said, "I will blot out man whom I have created from the face of the ground, man and beast and creeping things and birds of the air, for I am sorry that I have made them." 8 But Noah found favor in the eyes of the Lord. (Genesis 6:1,5-8)

God see the wickedness of man and decides to destroy all life on earth. He finds favor with Noah and his family and instructs him to build an ark. God gives Noah very specific instructions for the construction of the ark. Noah did all that God commanded. Then the time came to board the ark. Noah and his family along with seven pairs of all birds and animals. God gave Noah seven days to load the ark before it rained for forty days and forty nights. Again, Noah did all that God commanded.

And so it rained for forty days and forty nights, covering the earth, and even reaching to the mountains. No living thing survived except for Noah and his family, seven in total. After the flood ended, God sent a wind to dry up the land and the waters began to recede. After 150 days the ark rested on top of Mount Ararat. It took another six months for the waters to fully recede. Noah sent forth a dove to find dry land, but it returned with nothing. After seven days Noah sent it again, but it did not find dry land. After another seven, Noah sent the dove and it returned with an olive leaf. The waters had finally receded, there was dry land, and Noah left the ark.

Then Noah built an altar to the Lord, and took of every clean animal and of every clean bird, and offered burnt offerings on the altar. And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth. Every moving thing that lives shall be food for you; and as I gave you the green plants, I give you everything. 4 Only you shall not eat flesh with its life, that is, its blood. Whoever sheds the blood of man, by man shall his blood be shed; for God made man in his own image.

Genesis 9:9-17: "Behold, I establish my covenant with you and your descendants after you, and with every living creature that is with you, the birds, the cattle, and every beast of the earth with you, as many as came out of the ark. I establish my covenant with you, that never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of a flood, and never again shall there be a flood to destroy the earth." And God said, "This is the sign of the covenant which I make between me and you and every living creature that is with you, for all future generations: I set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and the earth."

Noah was the first tiller of the soil. He planted a vineyard; and he drank of the wine, and became drunk, and lay uncovered in his tent. And Ham, the father of Canaan, saw the nakedness of his father, and told his two brothers outside.

When Noah awoke and learned of what Ham did, he curse Ham's son Canaan, and blessed his other son Shem.

Noah is a reboot of Adam. Noah is told to be fruitful and multiply, to eat of everything with one exception, and made in the image of God. There is one exception -- the promise to never destroy the earth through flood. However, we will see the same pattern of humanity degenerating into sin. Beginning with the sons of Noah. One is sinful and the other is blessed.

These genealogies can be difficult to follow, but are very important in telling the story of Salvation History. Abraham is a descendant of Noah's son Shem. Abraham will be promised the land of Canaan, which descends from Noah's son Ham. Again we see a similar pattern of Cain and Abel, the City of God vs the City of Man, and the enmity between the two.

Noah was a descendant of Seth whereas Cain's lineage was destroyed in the flood. Next we will see how God redeems both through Abraham.

Abraham (Genesis 12-20; CCC 59-61, 2000-1700 BC)

11. Tower-of-Babel. The transition from Noah to Abraham is similar to the transition from Adam to Noah. The sons of God were intermarrying with the daughters of man and the earth became increasingly wicked. Instead of destroying the world like He did with the flood, God took a different path. People began building a tower to reach to the heavens (instead of climbing a mountain to meet God). Like reaching for the fruit from the tree, they were trying to become God without God. So God scattered the people and confused their languages. Hence, it was called Babel.

Chapter 11 ends with a list of descendants from Noah's son Shem to Abram. Shem is where we get the term Semites.

12. Calling of Abram. "Now the Lord said to Abram, "Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house to the land that I will show you. And I will make of you a great nation, and I will bless you, and make your name great, so that you will be a blessing. I will bless those who bless you, and him who curses you I will curse; and by you all the families of the earth shall bless themselves."

Abram obeyed God and left his land to the land that God promised. At the time, the Canaanites were in the land. Remember, Canaan was a descendant of Ham. Abram was always a sojourner in the land. Although he became wealthy and prosperous, he never conquered the land like Joshua would.

Then there was a famine in the land and Abram and Sarai traveled to Egypt. Again, we have this recurring theme of traveling to Egypt before entering the promised land.

14. Melchizedek. After rescuing his nephew Lot from captivity, we are introduced to a mysterious character, Melchizedek, who appears briefly in the Old Testament. Melchizedek was the priest-king of Salem. This is significant because this city become Jerusalem. Melchizedek brought out bread and wine and blessed Abram.

Hebrews 7 "For this Melchiz′edek, king of Salem, priest of the Most High God, met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him; ... He is ... king of righteousness, and then he is also king of Salem, that is, king of peace. He is without father or mother or genealogy, and has neither beginning of days nor end of life, but resembling the Son of God he continues a priest for ever."

"Be pleased to look upon these offerings with a serene and kindly countenance, and to accept them, as you were pleased to accept the gifts of your servant Abel the just, the sacrifice of Abraham, our father in faith, and the offering of your high priest Melchizedek, a holy sacrifice, a spotless victim." (Eucharistic Prayer)

17. Abram had received God's blessing and was promised that he would be the father of many nations. But decades had past and Abram was 99 years old. Then God elevated the promise to a covenant. The covenant sign would be circumcision. God renamed him Abraham and renamed his wife Sarah. He was promised a son to be named Isaac. One year later, Isaac was born when Abraham was 100 years old.

22. Binding of Isaac. Years later, God asked of Abraham to sacrifice his only son, Isaac. He took Isaac to Mount Moriah. Isaac was a young man at this time. We know this because Isaac carried the wood up the mountain for the sacrifice. This is a precursor of Jesus carrying the wood of the cross up Mount Calvary. On the way up, Isaac asked where is the lamb. Abraham answered, God will provide the lamb. We learn in the New Testament that Abraham believed God would raise Isaac from the dead. Then at the last second, an angel appeared and stopped Abraham from sacrificing his son. Then he saw a ram in the thicket and offered it to the Lord. But the question still remained, where is the lamb, and the prophecy unfulfilled until Jesus that God would provide. As announced by Saint John the Baptist. "Behold the Lamb of God".

Mount Moriah is the site that would become become the sight of Solomon's Temple.

24-41. Isaac marries Rebekah and has twin sons, Jacob and Esau. Jacob receives the blessing, he a later renamed Israel, and has 12 sons. Jacob shows favoritism to his son Joseph, and his brothers sell him into slavery. Joseph miraculously becomes the second in command in Egypt. Once again, there is a famine in the land and the 12 brothers are forced to travel to Egypt for food. There, they are reunited with their brother Joseph.

Next is the story of Moses. This concludes the book of Genesis and the story of Moses picks up in the book of Exodus.

Moses (Exodus 1-40; CCC 62-64)

There is no major event like the Flood or the Tower of Babel to introduce the next character. Exodus begins with the death of Joseph, the Israelites were fruitful, increased greatly, and grew exceedingly strong over several generations, so that the land was filled with them.

Then a king came along who was not familiar with Joseph nor sympathetic to his people. He began to oppress them in order to slow or stop their growth. He ordered midwives to kill Hebrew boys as soon as they are born. But the midwives feared God and disobeyed the king. The Hebrews continued to increase and grow strong. Then he commanded his people to throw every Hebrew male child into the Nile River.

This sets the stage for Moses and his leadership out of Egypt. Moses was from the tribe of Levi. His mom put him in an ark in the river. He was found by the Pharaoh's daughter and later adopted as her son. Moses grew up in the Pharaoh's house.

After many years while Moses was shepherding the flock of his father-in-law, God appeared to him in a burning bush on Mount Horeb. The bush was on fire but was not being consumed by the flames. As Moses approached it to discern what was happening, God spoke to Him saying that He is the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, He has heard the cry of His people and wants Moses to resue them. Moses replie, Who I am that I should go to Pharaoh and deliver the people from him? God reassures Mosses that He will be with Him and then reveals His divine name, I AM WHO I AM. Tell Pharoah that I AM has sent you.

The Pharaoh refuses to listen to Moses, even though Moses performed miraculous signs and wonders. So God sent 10 plagues upon Egypt. Each plague was progressively worse until the last one, which was the killing of the firstborn son of each household. The story comes full circle as this is how we are introduced to the Pharaoh. He feared the growth and strength of the Israelites and tried to kill the firstborn son of every Israelite. In a Divine twist of events, God honors his wish. Instead of targeting only the Israelites, God targeted everyone who lived in Egypt, but he provided a way out.

12-13. "The Lord said to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, 2 "This month shall be for you the beginning of months; it shall be the first month of the year for you. 3 Tell all the congregation of Israel that on the tenth day of this month they shall take every man a lamb according to their fathers' houses, a lamb for a household; 4 and if the household is too small for a lamb, then a man and his neighbor next to his house shall take according to the number of persons; according to what each can eat you shall make your count for the lamb. 5 Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male a year old; you shall take it from the sheep or from the goats; 6 and you shall keep it until the fourteenth day of this month, when the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill their lambs in the evening.[b] 7 Then they shall take some of the blood, and put it on the two doorposts and the lintel of the houses in which they eat them. 8 They shall eat the flesh that night, roasted; with unleavened bread and bitter herbs they shall eat it. 9 Do not eat any of it raw or boiled with water, but roasted, its head with its legs and its inner parts. 10 And you shall let none of it remain until the morning, anything that remains until the morning you shall burn. 11 In this manner you shall eat it: your loins girded, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand; and you shall eat it in haste. It is the Lord's passover. 12 For I will pass through the land of Egypt that night, and I will smite all the first-born in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and on all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am the Lord. 13 The blood shall be a sign for you, upon the houses where you are; and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague shall fall upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt. 14 "This day shall be for you a memorial day, and you shall keep it as a feast to the Lord; throughout your generations you shall observe it as an ordinance for ever."

14-15. The Pharaoh finally let the people go in his moment of grief. His heart quickly hardened again and he sent his army after the Israelites. But through Moses, God parted the Red Sea and the Israelites escaped Egypt on dry land. God then closed the walls of water on the Egyptian army.

The Israelites spent forty year in the desert before reachgin the Promised land. During the desert wanderings, they were provided food in the form of miraculous bread from heaven, called Manna. He also provided water from the rock at Meribah.

Then in Chapter 20, God gives Moses the Ten Commandments on Mount Sinai. Mount Sinai is the same as Mount Horeb where Moses encountered God in the burning bush. These were not the only laws that the Israelites had to keep as a part of the covenant. The next three chapters lay out those laws.

Chapters 24-37 provide instructions for the construction of the tabernacle and the ark of the covenant. The tabernacle was a moveable structure in which the Ark of the Covenant was kept. It was the place of worship and the presence of God.

Moses was not allowed to enter the promised land and could only see it from distance. Joshua was the next major Old Testament book named for the person who would conquer Canaan and take possession of the land. He divided it among the 12 tribes. They were governed by judges, the the people longed for a king.

Of notable mention between Joshua and King David is **Ruth. She is in the lineage of Jesus and is where Bethlehem enters the picture.

King David (1 & 2 Samuel; CCC 64)

Daivd was anointed by Samuel, the last judge, and begins a new era for the Israelites. One of the promises to Abraham is fulfilled in becoming a kingdom. David was the youngest son of Jesse, and was chosen by God and anointed by the prophet Samuel while still a shepherd boy.

David earned a decisive victory in the battle with Goliath. Armed only with a sling and stones, David defeated the Philistine giant Goliath, declaring "The battle is the Lord's". The less-competent King Saul feels threatened by David's victory and David winning honor among the people.

After Saul's death, David was first crowned king of Judah, then king over all Israel. His reign unified the tribes and expanded Israel's territory.

David earned another decisive victory by conquering Jerusalem and making it his capital. Jerusalem was later called the "City of David". This established Jerusalem as both political and spiritual center. As the spiritual center, it would become the place for the Temple and important for understanding Jesus' life, death, and resurrection.

One of the key events of David's kingship was bringing the Ark of the Covenant to Jerusalem. David carried the Ark from hill country of Judah to Jerusalem with great celebration, establishing it as the religious heart of Israel. This act symbolized God's presence among His people. However, along the way, the ark paused for three months after a tragic incident. In the Mosaic law, the Ark was to be carried only by the Levites and on their shoulders by two poles in rings on the Ark. One of the men thought of a better way to carry the ark by using a cart with oxen. But the oxen stumbled and the Ark began to fall. One of the men, reached out to stabilize it, and when he touched it, he died.

There are striking parallels between this story and the Visitation of Mary to Elizabeth. The Church has taught that Mary is the New Ark of the Covenant not only because of this parallel but several others.

God makes a Covenant with with David and promised David that his dynasty would endure forever, a covenant fulfilled in the messianic lineage.

Even though David sinned with Bathsheba and murdered her husband, Uriah he repented and was forgiven. However, David's sins had temporal consequences and brought lasting consequences to his household.

Later, David faced family turmoil, including rebellion of one of his sons. Despite failures, he remained "a man after God's own heart" (Acts 13:22), remembered for his psalms, leadership, and covenant with God.
