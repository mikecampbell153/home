Thus becoming full members of the Catholic Church.

In typical Catholic fashion, we never just to the thing, we always have some type of an introduction. This makes sense on a practical level because the Church's sacraments and rites are typically very short prayers. I baptize you in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, Be sealed with the gifts of the Holy Spirit, and this is my body.

But the Church also sees the importance of the entire community embracing our newest members. We've been learning the faith, or to use the fancy word catechesis, for the past few months in this small OCIA community. Then we send you to the larger parish community. Then the parish sends you to the larger diocesan community.

The Rite of Election is an ancient rite of the Church going back centuries. It has been renewed recently and the basic formula has remained consistent throughout the centuries. There is a period of Inquiry, then a period of Catechesis, then a period of Purification and Enlightenment, and lastly a period of pedagogy.

This process used to last for a few years. A little Catholic trivia -- One of the functions of this rite was to protect the church from hostile infiltrators during times of persecution during the first centuries of the Church. The Roman Empire was a surveillance state that would spy on Christians, betray them through informants, arrest Mass-goers at house gatherings, and execute Christians who refused to worship the Emperor. The Church had to be especially careful with who was allowed to participate in Mass.

This was the practical side and the secondary function of the Rite of Election. The more important function is the spiritual aspect which is authentic conversion, becoming part of a community of believers, and prayer and preparation for the Sacraments.

Going back to our recent Rite of Election, it was not only special because of the presence of the Archbishop and other parishes, but it was a turning point. The OCIA book calls it the hinge of this whole process. We transition from Catechesis to Purification and Enlightenment. You are no longer called Catechumens. Good news! You are now the Elect.

A couple of points of emphasis. God has chosen you. The Church ratified that election. That bears repeating, God has chosen you. As the Archbishop said, God likely acted through friends, or family, or co-workers. God loves us so much that He chooses His creatures to evangelize and minister to each other. He could do it all Himself, but He chooses us to carry out His mission. Even in the Annunication to the Blessed Virgin Mary, He chose Saint Gabriel to have the Supreme honor of delivering the message of the Incarnation. You have been chosen by God and now called the Elect.

This Archbishop asked if you have faithfully listened to God's Word. And you have. Have you begun to walk in God's presence? You have. Are you ready for the sacraments of initiation. You are.

He then addressed the Candidates, who have already been baptized, and welcomed you to full participation in the Church this coming Easter weekend.

The Archbishop then prayed for your protection, for perseverance, for purification, and for deeper conversion.

Saying Yes to Jesus

Slide - Mary washing Jesus' feet

On to tonight's session titled "Saying Yes to Jesus". What are we saying Yes to? Mary said yes to God at the annunciaiton, "Be it done unto me according to thy word." Jesus said yes to the Father in the Garden of Gethseme, "Let this cup pass from me, but not my will but Thine be done." In short, we are saying yes to being a disciple of Jesus Christ.

A disciple is one who follows Jesus. What does it mean to follow Jesus? What is God asking of us to follow Him? Here are five things from the Gospels:

  • Loving Jesus more than immediate family (Luke 14:26)
  • Self-denial, complete dedication, willing obedience, and total commitment - even unto death (Luke 14:27)
  • Surrenders everything for Jesus (Luke 14:33)
  • Remains true to Jesus' words and teachings (John 8:31)
  • Love others as Jesus has loved him. (John 13:34-35)

The Catechism states, "The disciple of Christ must not only keep the faith and live on it, but also profess it, confidently bear witness to it, and spread it" (CCC 1816). Satan was first to disobey, then Even, the Adam. The key to being a disciple is obedience.

Saint Mary Magdalene

As we move into this next phase from learning and catechesis to purification and enlightenment, from the Catechumenate to the Elect, I thought we could walk through the story of one of the greatest disciples, Saint Mary Magdalene, and do so in light of the seven mansions of Saint Teresa of Avila.

Saint Teresa of Avila was a Carmelite nun who lived during the counter-reformation. She was significant in reforming the Church from within and became the first female doctor of the Church. One of her most important works was a book titled The Interior Castle. She used a castle with many rooms as an analogy of the soul. The soul is a vast castle with many rooms, and God dwells in the center, the holy of holies.

Saint Paul teaches us in Scripture that we are temples of the Holy Spirit. We also read of God living within us. This notion of God being in us is an analogy of personal relationship. As we grow in that personal relationship, or in discipleship, we enter into successive rooms until reaching union with God. We may not always feel God's presence in our lives, dwelling in our souls, but He is there. This analogy of an interior castle is an analogy of unlocking each room as we grow closer to God in our spiritual journeys.

Her seven mansions are in line with Carmel spirituality and growth which can be simplified into three ways: the purgative way, the Illuminative Way, the Unitive way.

This might be ambitious, but I though we could journey through these seven mansions the the Biblical encounters with Saint Mary Magdalene.

Mary Magdalene is only person mentioned in all four gospels with the exception of Jesus and His mother, the Blessed Virgin Mary, obviously, and six of the apostles. Not even Saint John the Baptist, nor Saint Joseph are explicitly named in the four gospels. (The apostles? Peter, Andrew, James, John, Thomas, and Judas).

Magdalene is a title meaning tower. A tower fits in extremely well with the analogy of a castle. Furthermore, it provides a beautiful analogy of the ascent of the soul. We will see Mary go from a sinful women, who was possessed by seven demons, to the first witness of the resurrection.

Before we start, I want to point out that this is the teaching of the saints, not official Church teaching. I am drawing primarily from Pope Gregory the Great's teaching on Mary Magdalene and Saint Teresa of Avila. If you disagree with this approach, that is okay, and I encourage you to do your own research.

Wet His feet with her tears

Two notable themes throughout Mary's story is feet and tears. Mary is always pictured at Jesus' feet and often weeping in tears. She models true discipleship by serving at the feet of Jesus. Jesus models this same act with the Apostles at the Last Supper.

Quick plug for Holy Thursday Mass. While it is an honor to be asked to participate, it is an extremely humbling act. It is an act of mutual humility.

The other theme is tears. We will see tears of sorrow, tears of compassion, tears of loss, tears of grief, and tears of joy. Tears can be a gift from God. They can be consoling and cleansing.

Saint Bernard said, "The tears of penitents, are the wine of angels." (Lapide)

For she loved much

Mary comes to Jesus out of need. Having been possessed by seven demons, she is likely suffering from the sin of despair having lost all hope. She recognizes her need for forgiveness. After Jesus cleanses her of the seven demons and forgives her, she responds with great love by washing His feet with her tears and drying them with her hair. It is ultimately a mystery of how we can love God, but it is best demonstrated in how we love others. Jesus said the two greatest commands are to love God and love neighbor as self.

The Catechism defines love, "'To love is to will the good of another.' All other affections have their source in this first movement of the human heart toward the good. Again, love is to will the good of another." (CCC 1766) Being made in the image and likeness of God, we were created with an intellect and a will. With our intellect, we are not only self-aware but also aware of the needs of others. It is an act of the intellect, knowing what others need, and an act of the will to satisfy those needs. Love is an act of the will for the common good of our neighbor.

Mary anointed Jesus' feet at the house of a Pharisee, who "said to himself, if he were a prophet he would know who is touching his feet, for she is a sinner." Although he said it to himself, Jesus answered him with a parable about two men in debt. The one with the larger debt loves more. Therefore I tell you, her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much; but he who is forgiven little, loves little."

I think Jesus' parable is a lesson in humility and contrition.

Who is this, who even forgives sins?

Who is this who can forgive sins? This is a great question. Only God can forgive sins. In this first encounter with Jesus, Mary Magdalene and those present are being challenged to contemplate Who is this? Jesus is the Eternal Word Who took on human flesh. He is not, never was, and never will be a human person. He is from all eternity a Divine Person, the second Person of the Most Holy Trinity. At the fullness of time, He took on human form becoming man. He is one Person; two natures and two wills, human nature and divine nature.

The Catechism teaches, "Only God can forgive sins, but Jesus willed that the Church should be the instrument of forgiveness on earth. On the night of that first Easter Sunday, Jesus breathed on the apostles and said, "As the Father has sent me, so I send you ... Receive the Holy Spirit. Whose sins you forgive are forgiven them, and whose sins you retain are retained." (John 20:21-23) That night Jesus gave the Church the ministry of forgiveness of sins through the apostles. (cf CCC)

"Confession liberates us from sins that trouble our hearts and makes it possible to be reconciled to God and others."

By not only healing people, exorcising demons, and raising people from the dead, Jesus also forgave people of their sins. The miracles were outward signs of Jesus' divinity, forgiving them of their sins was a proclamation of His divinity. His words truly resulted in complete forgiveness and by His words was Jesus claiming to be God.

It reminds me of the finding of the boy Jesus in the Temple when He was 12 or 13 years old. On the way back from Jerusalem, Mary and Joseph realized that Jesus was not with them. Upon finding Him in the Temple after being lost for three days, Jesus asked his parents, "Did you not know that I would be at my Father's house?" By saying this, Jesus was claiming to be equal to God the Father. Later on He said this again, the Pharisees accused Him of blasphemy, and sought to put Him to death. It makes me wonder, Did Joseph realize at that moment that His Son, Jesus, by claiming to be God would be put to death?

took hold of his feet, and worshiped him

Worship is reserved for God alone. The Church uses the term latria for Worship of God, and dulia, or veneration, for honor and respect given to the saints. Mary is given highest respect, or hyperdulia and Saint Joseph given next highest with proto-dulia. But true worship, which always involves sacrifice, is reserver for God and God alone.

Falling at His feet is a sign of adoration. The holy women show us the example of how to approach Jesus at Eucharistic Adoration, bowing with both knees, if physically able because we acknowledge the Real, True, and Substantial Presence of Jesus in the Most Blessed Sacrament. At minimum, the Church asks us to genuflect and we do before the tabernacle.

Worship is not a sentimental act, but one of justice. Once we recognize and believe that Jesus is God, that through Him all things were made; we recognize that we owe everything to Him. That is, giving God what is due to Him. Worship is an obligation, a virtue, and an act of faith. The Eucharist is a sacrifice, not just a meal. It is the one and same Sacrifice on Calvary made sacramentally present in an unbloody manner. It is the source and summit of Catholic life.

Augustine writes: "To worship God is to acknowledge Him as God."

Salvation History

Intro

Road to Emmaus (Luke 24:27)

The Pentateuch comprises the first five books of the Old Testament. It depicts a series of beginnings—the beginning of the world, of humankind, and of God's promise to the Israelites.

Genesis, the first book, opens with God's creation of the world. The perfect world falls into evil when humans disobey God, and the human population divides into separate nations and languages.

There are many covenants in the Bible, some between people and some between God and people. Covenant comes from the Latin word "convenire" which means to come together. A covenant is an agreement between people, or between God and His people, which binds one to the other. This is vastly different to a contract, which involves the exchange of goods or services for money.

A covenant can be thought of as a sacred family bond, taken with an oath before a higher power, whereas a contract can be thought of as an agreement to pay someone for services rendered, which involves a promise to each other, rather than an oath. BIG DIFFERENCE. Covenants most often involve a ceremony with witnesses, invoking God, and a communal meal. The covenant therefore unites flesh and blood, as well as ensuring a common family name.

There are five covenants in the Old Testament which are fulfilled in the New Testament, or New Covenant when Jesus establishes the Church as the Kingdom of God. Each covenant in the Old Testament expands the relationships from one couple, Adam & Eve, to one family, Noah, to 12 tribes Abraham, to one nation, Moses, and finally one kingdom, David.

The Old Testament is one cohesive story with many pivotal chapters of God revealing Himself to man through covenants that culminate in a kingdom. Like any good story, there is plenty of conflict and resolutions to the conflict. It is one redemption arc from Adam to Jesus, the new Adam. Within this one overarching arc, there are many other redemption arcs with captivating and compelling stories.

These arcs follow the mysteries of the Rosary, the Life of Christ... Joyful, Sorrowful, and Glorious. Faith, sin, and redemption. Time and time again, God comes to meet man, man sins and falls away from God, and God redeems man. Hence the image of a mountain. God comes to meet man on earth while man climbs the mountain to meet God. This is the definition of prayer... Raising the heart to God.

Matthew 16:18-19 Upon this rock, I will build my church... I will give you the keys to the kingdom.

Adam & Eve (Genesis 1-5; CCC 396-421)

Genesis 1 and 2 provide two different accounts of Creation.

Genesis One provides an account in which Creation took place over six days plus a day of rest. This day of rest is important in the history of salvation because it establishes God's covenant with man. It is not explicitly stated but the idea of rest and the number seven unfold as symbols of covenant.

This first account of creation has beautiful symmetry with two sets of three. The first three; light, sea, and land are filled by the second set sun/moon, fish/birds, and animals/man. The sixth day, the day that man is created, is called very good.

The sixth day is very good because of the creation of man, which is expanded on in the second creation account. The first creation account ends on the seventh day with God resting, establishing the Sabbath Day. It ends on a high note. "And he blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made." (Gen 2:3)

The second creation account is focused on the creation of man, male and female. God created man from the dust of the earth, breathed life into him, and he became of living soul (2:7). God placed the man in the Garden of Eden, which is described as paradise with plants, herbs, trees, and flowing rivers. There are two trees mentioned specifically, the tree of life and the tree of knowledge. God told Adam that he could eat of any tree, except the tree of knowledge of good and evil. Then the Lord God said, "It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him a helper fit for him." (2:18) But no suitable helper fit for him. So God put Adam into a deep sleep and from his rib, He created woman. Adam said, "This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man."

Then the devil enters the picture. God allows this test of obedience because we are made in his image and likeness with intellect and free will. Love is not love unless it is free. God will never violate our free will and coerce us to do anything. The serpent lied to Eve and she ate of the forbidden fruit. She gave it to Adam, and he ate as well. Original sin.

God's response to sin is that He comes to meet us (the Incarnation), He provides a means of repentance (the Passion), and a plan of salvation (the Resurrection). God curses the serpent putting enmity between he and the woman, his offspring and hers. We will see this enmity throughout Salvation History. We will see these two themes throughout Salvation History. Faith, Fall or sin, Forgiveness or redemption. But not all seek forgiveness resulting is separation between City of God and City of Man, as Saint Augustine describes it.

By grace, God banished Adam and Eve from the Garden of Eden and put an angel to protect the garden. This was to prevent them from eating from the tree of life and living forever in a state of sin.

Immediately after the fall, we see in the first generation after Adam and Eve, with their first two sons, the effects of sin. Abel's sacrifice is accepted but Cain's is not. And this made him angry. God tells Cain, "Why are you angry, and why has your countenance fallen? 7 If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin is couching at the door; its desire is for you, but you must master it." Instead Cain kills Abel. Instead of turning from sin and repenting, Cain's anger grew to the point of killing his own brother, Abel.

Moses (Exodus 1-40; CCC 62-64)

There is no major event like the Flood or the Tower of Babel to introduce the next character. Exodus begins with the death of Joseph, the Israelites were fruitful, increased greatly, and grew exceedingly strong over several generations, so that the land was filled with them.

Then he commanded his people to throw every Hebrew male child into the Nile River.

This sets the stage for Moses and his leadership out of Egypt. Moses was from the tribe of Levi. His mom put him in an ark in the river. He was found by the Pharaoh's daughter and later adopted as her son. Moses grew up in the Pharaoh's house.

After many years while Moses was shepherding the flock of his father-in-law, God appeared to him in a burning bush on Mount Horeb. The bush was on fire but was not being consumed by the flames. King David (1 & 2 Samuel; CCC 64)

Daivd was anointed by Samuel, the last judge, and begins a new era for the Israelites. One of the promises to Abraham is fulfilled in becoming a kingdom. David was the youngest son of Jesse, and was chosen by God and anointed by the prophet Samuel while still a shepherd boy.

David earned a decisive victory in the battle with Goliath. Armed only with a sling and stones, David defeated the Philistine giant Goliath, declaring "The battle is the Lord's". The less-competent King Saul feels threatened by David's victory and David winning honor among the people.

After Saul's death, David was first crowned king of Judah, then king over all Israel. His reign unified the tribes and expanded Israel's territory.

David earned another decisive victory by conquering Jerusalem and making it his capital. Jerusalem was later called the "City of David". This established Jerusalem as both political and spiritual center. As the spiritual center, it would become the place for the Temple and important for understanding Jesus' life, death, and resurrection.

One of the key events of David's kingship was bringing the Ark of the Covenant to Jerusalem. David carried the Ark from hill country of Judah to Jerusalem with great celebration, establishing it as the religious heart of Israel. This act symbolized God's presence among His people. However, along the way, the ark paused for three months after a tragic incident. In the Mosaic law, the Ark was to be carried only by the Levites and on their shoulders by two poles in rings on the Ark. One of the men thought of a better way to carry the ark by using a cart with oxen. But the oxen stumbled and the Ark began to fall. One of the men, reached out to stabilize it, and when he touched it, he died.

There are striking parallels between this story and the Visitation of Mary to Elizabeth. The Church has taught that Mary is the New Ark of the Covenant not only because of this parallel but several others.

God makes a Covenant with with David and promised David that his dynasty would endure forever, a covenant fulfilled in the messianic lineage.

Later, David faced family turmoil, including rebellion of one of his sons. Despite failures, he remained "a man after God's own heart" (Acts 13:22), remembered for his psalms, leadership, and covenant with God.
